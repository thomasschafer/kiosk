     traversal to retrieve them in sorted order.
   * Bitonic Sort: A parallel algorithm for sorting, often used in hardware implementations.
   * Bogosort: A purely theoretical (and humorous) algorithm that randomly shuffles the list until
     it happens to be sorted. Its average complexity is $O(n \cdot n!)$, making it the gold standard
     for inefficiency.
   * Sleep Sort: A curiosity where a thread is started for each element x, and each thread sleeps
     for x seconds before printing x.


  Conclusion
  No single sorting algorithm is superior in every scenario. The "best" choice depends on the
  specific constraints: Merge Sort is preferred for stability and linked lists; Quick Sort for
  general-purpose internal sorting; Insertion Sort for small or nearly sorted arrays; and
  Radix/Counting Sort for large sets of integers with a limited range. Modern hybrids like Timsort
  and IntroSort provide a robust "best-of-both-worlds" solution for the majority of software
  engineering tasks.
